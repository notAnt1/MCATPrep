# MCAT website topic map

Version 1.0.0 | Reviewed October 9, 2026

**26 navigation groups, 128 selectable topics, and 525 expandable subtopics.** The default heat map uses the 128 topic cells, grouped and filtered by section. Subtopics appear only when expanded. The 938 coverage concepts are search/scope detail, not another flat checklist or another default heat map.

**AAMC-grounded, not AAMC-endorsed.** The official outline supplies scope and skills; the student-friendly grouping is our product design. Coverage includes all 31 science categories and the three CARS skill families. The finite taxonomy covers published required content and reasoning dimensions, not every possible research context or future change.

[Official AAMC outline](https://students-residents.aamc.org/prepare-mcat-exam/whats-mcat-exam-pdf-outline) | [Official CARS overview](https://students-residents.aamc.org/whats-mcat-exam/critical-analysis-and-reasoning-skills-section-overview)

## How students navigate

Section filter → topic group → topic → optional subtopic. Example: B/B → Cell biology → Membrane transport → Active transport. One canonical ID is reused wherever a topic appears across sections. A student can select a whole topic or a narrower child. Selection fetches questions testing that target, not questions that merely mention it.

A group heading, a source category, a learning topic, and a reasoning skill are distinct. Topic-level category lists are possible mappings; they are not automatically attached wholesale to each question. CARS task names likewise do not determine the official skill by themselves.

## Scope and overlap rules

- C/P and B/B share many molecules, methods, and physical principles. Reuse canonical concepts and allow section-specific performance views. A category candidate only applies when its particular content is actually tested.
- Nervous-system topics can be used in B/B or P/S; record the question context and necessary knowledge. Conventional electrolysis does not inherit nervous-system category 3A merely because the parent electrochemistry topic also contains Nernst-related physiology.
- In CARS, main idea, tone, structure, and inference may involve comprehension or reasoning within the text. Assign the official skill from the evidence scope required, not the stem keyword. Applying ideas or evaluating new information can test reasoning beyond the text.
- Research and quantitative topics are cross-cutting practice choices. Keep them separate from the official four science skill codes; they do not create additional AAMC skills.
- Incidental or unfamiliar research entities remain context tags. Do not create a false knowledge deficit from something supplied in the passage.
- The JSON contains scope concepts and search aliases, including enantiomeric excess, Beer-Lambert law, SDS-PAGE, and pathway abbreviations. These synonyms are navigation aids, not claims that each term is an individually named official category.

## CARS passage subjects are optional context filters

The outline gives nonexhaustive examples of humanities and social-science disciplines. Use them to vary reading practice; do not label a learner weak in philosophy or history knowledge from a CARS miss.

**Humanities examples:** Architecture, Art, Dance, Ethics, Literature, Music, Philosophy, Popular culture, Religion, Theater, Studies of diverse cultures.

**Social-science examples:** Anthropology, Archaeology, Economics, Education, Geography, History, Linguistics, Political science, Population health, Psychology, Sociology, Studies of diverse cultures.
